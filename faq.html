Frequently Asked Questions

Straight answers to the questions people ask Rockland County Real Estate most about realtor in Nanuet, NY.

How does buying a home work in New York compared to other states?

In New York, an accepted offer is not a signed deal yet. After you agree on terms, the seller's attorney drafts a contract and your attorney reviews it. The deal is usually binding once both sides sign the contract and the buyer's deposit is made. We help you through each step and keep both attorneys in the loop.

Do I need a real estate attorney in Rockland County?

Most buyers and sellers in Rockland County use a real estate attorney to handle the contract and closing. We can share names of local attorneys our clients have worked with, and we coordinate with them throughout the deal.

How do property taxes work here?

Property taxes in Rockland County include county, town, school district and sometimes village taxes. They can vary a lot from one town or school district to the next, even between neighboring streets. We help you look up the current taxes for any home you are considering, and you can ask the town assessor about exemptions like STAR.

How do I know which school district a home is in?

School district lines in Rockland do not always match the town name in the mailing address. A home with a Nanuet or West Nyack address, for example, might fall in a different district than you expect. We confirm the district for every home you are serious about.

What should I watch for when buying an older home in Rockland?

Common items include old oil tanks, original electrical panels, aging boilers, roof age and water in basements. Some areas also sit near mapped flood zones. A good home inspection plus a few smart questions for the seller will cover most of it, and we help you understand the findings.

What does a seller need to disclose in New York?

New York sellers of most residential homes are required to give buyers a Property Condition Disclosure Statement before the contract is signed. It covers things like known water problems, the age of systems and flood history. Your attorney can explain exactly what applies to your sale.

How long does it take to close on a home?

It depends on financing, the attorneys and the inspection results, but many sales close roughly two to three months after the offer is accepted. Cash deals can move faster. We give you a realistic timeline at the start and keep it updated.

How do you decide the right price for my home?

We look at recent sales of similar homes nearby, homes currently for sale and homes that did not sell, then adjust for your home's size, condition, updates and lot. You get a clear explanation of how we got to the number, so you can decide with confidence.
